# BrightHome prospect presentation — 3 to 5 minutes

Run `rehearse.py --present` using OPERATOR-RUNBOOK.md. Scroll to heading 1 before starting.
Read these cues aloud and show the matching numbered scene.
Use the offline rehearsal output for this milestone. Say plainly that it is a tool
walkthrough; existing voice acceptance is separate evidence. Do not stretch a
120-second capped voice session to cover the whole presentation.

**0:00–0:25 — Introduce the problem.**
“When your team is busy, callers can leave before anyone answers. This is Autumn Winters, an AI Customer Experience Specialist demonstrating Crew by AW Creatives for fictional BrightHome. The business supplies
the facts; Autumn can explain services, obtain an estimate and prepare an appointment.
Crew is a Digital Workforce platform. Clients receive specialists customized for their business, not a copy of Autumn. This walkthrough uses synthetic data and does not make real bookings.”

Show the configured greeting naming Autumn and BrightHome.

**0:25–1:15 — Explain services.**
Caller: “What services do you offer?” Show the six configured offerings and two add-ons.
Caller: “What does deep cleaning include, and how is it different from standard?”
Show included/excluded work. “Does that include inside the oven?” Explain the controlled
add-on. “Do you clean swimming pools?” Show the verified-knowledge boundary and offer
human follow-up without inventing availability or a price.

**1:15–1:45 — FAQ and interruption.**
Caller: “What hours are you open?” Then interrupt: “Actually, are you insured?”
For this offline walkthrough, show the insurance tool answer and explain that these
are fictional configured facts. Mention that a prior human voice test passed
barge-in; do not claim the offline output itself proves audible interruption.
A future separately authorized live excerpt can demonstrate this interaction.

**1:45–2:25 — Deterministic estimate.**
Caller: “What would deep cleaning for three bedrooms cost?” Show the tool's $225 demo
estimate. Standard three-bedroom cleaning is $145. Catalog descriptions do not create
prices: other services and add-ons need human review, and these are not real offers.

**2:25–3:15 — Safe booking and confirmation.**
Show the fixed synthetic slots. Caller: “Please prepare the first appointment.”
Explain that a proposal is not a booking. Explain that caller-only confirmation is blocked (checked by the fixture); show
explicit local operator approval, and the resulting confirmed synthetic slot. Show
that attempting to book that same slot again is rejected. Show the expired second proposal: no second booking was made; the first remains. Explain that a stale review
requires permission and a fresh availability check, not an automatic re-offer.

**3:15–4:00 — Human follow-up and summary reveal.**
Show the local sales handoff: context is retained, but no person is connected.
Show the synthetic transcript excerpt; this is tool-walkthrough text, not recorded speech.
Caller: “I would like a person to follow up.” Reveal the retained estimate, confirmed
mock booking and local human-follow-up record. “No person has been contacted in this
demo. The business can see what happened instead of receiving a context-free lead.”

**4:00–4:30 — Close.**
“AWC controls business facts, quote rules, approval checks and context records.
Retell supplies managed voice infrastructure; this walkthrough does not play audio.
For a real business, we would configure its verified services, policies and approval
rules, then validate its integrations. This is a bounded demonstration, not a deployed
phone receptionist.” Stop here; no automatic outreach or production rollout.
