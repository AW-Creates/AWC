# Short discovery and factual objection handling

## Response-driven fit conversation: about 8–12 minutes

Ask permission for a few questions. Start with the owner's account, then follow the relevant branch; do not interrogate through every item if there is no fit.

1. “Walk me through what happens when someone calls for cleaning while you're on a job.” If already handled well, ask whether anything remains frustrating. If no problem, stop the pitch.
2. “Roughly how many inbound inquiries arrive in a typical week? How many are routine questions? What happens after hours?” Record owner estimate and uncertainty; do not infer volume from reviews. Would 20 eligible calls in 14 days be plausible?
3. “Who answers, how quickly can someone call back, and what interruptions take the most time?” Ask for a typical example and optional aggregate baseline, not caller records.
4. “Which three questions repeat most? Which answers must always come from you?” Identify approved facts versus defer-to-human cases.
5. “How do you estimate standard and deep cleaning? What inputs change the price, and when must someone inspect?” If no bounded rules, hold or explicitly narrow; never invent rules from public prices.
6. “Which calendar is authoritative? Who approves a booking? Are durations, travel or crew assignments complex?” Complex dispatch is out of first-pilot scope. No integration promise on a discovery call.
7. “Which calls must reach a person, and who is primary and backup? What callback timing can you actually meet?” Live transfer is excluded. No reliable fallback means no pilot.
8. “What would make two weeks worthwhile: fewer interruptions, more complete requests, or more timely first responses? What would show it did not work?” Record baseline, observable target and unacceptable errors. Use only the owner's numbers.
9. “Could you approve the facts, review outcomes daily and join day 3/7/14 reviews? Would a paid pilot with setup and capped minutes be worth discussing after feasibility?” Unknown price is not hidden; say it is not set yet.

Summarize back: pain, evidence, preferred overflow OR after-hours path, desired outcome, human boundaries and unresolved facts. Ask whether that accurately describes the need. Let the owner correct or decline.

**Disposition:** no pain/non-fit → close respectfully; unknown key facts → request only the needed information; fit + interest → conditional feasibility/intake, no purchase or integration authority implied. Commercial acceptance is not live launch authorization.

Record: owner-confirmed baseline, objective, call-volume estimate, estimate rules, calendar/approval owner, primary/backup, constraints, pilot interest and agreed next step/date. Keep real contact information and private business answers outside public Git.

## Objections

| Concern | Factual response | Discovery / next step |
|---|---|---|
| Customers want a human | That may be right for some callers. The proposed pilot identifies itself as AI and limits routine handling; a person handles exceptions. Today's demo only creates a local follow-up record. Delivered escalation must be built and tested; instant transfer is excluded. | Which calls should always go to your team? If callers reject the approach, use that as a stop signal. |
| AI sounds robotic | Voice quality is subjective. Previous voice/barge-in tests passed, but the offline walkthrough cannot prove how it sounds. No promise every caller will like it. | Offer a separately authorized short voice excerpt later; ask what would feel unacceptable. |
| It might make mistakes | It can. The demo constrains facts and estimate rules and defers unsupported pricing. A live pilot still needs approved facts, acceptance tests, monitoring and fallback. | Identify high-consequence answers; keep them human-only. Pause for a wrong price or unsafe action. |
| Wrong booking | The local demo separates proposals from operator approval and rejects conflict/stale cases. A real calendar and authenticated approval are not implemented for the pilot yet. | Confirm one calendar and approver; otherwise explicitly agree request-only scope or decline. |
| Voicemail is enough | If it works and follow-up is reliable, a pilot may add little. The potential difference is approved answers and structured context before callback. | What, if anything, is missing in current messages? Do not force a replacement. |
| Why not a receptionist? | A person handles judgment and relationships. AWC's proposed role is bounded routine overflow or after-hours coverage. No staffing reduction or cheaper-than-staff claim is proven. | Compare the actual uncovered workflow, costs and preferred caller experience. |
| Price concern | No final price has been chosen. The proposal is a fixed paid 14-day pilot with setup and capped minutes; feasibility and effort determine the quote. No unlimited usage, automatic renewal or return-on-investment guarantee. | What improvement would justify a test? If it cannot justify its cost, stop. Do not quote voice-only cost as total cost. |
| What if it goes down? | Provider/routing outages are possible; there is no uptime SLA or 24/7 support promise. Verified routing fallback, monitoring and human/voicemail recovery are launch prerequisites. | Agree and test where calls go on failure before go-live; if fallback cannot be verified, do not launch. |

Never promise refunds, free live trials, instant setup, integrations, conversion lift or availability merely to overcome an objection. An unresolved concern is evidence to hold or decline, not pressure to close.
