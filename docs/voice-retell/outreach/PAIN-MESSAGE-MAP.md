## Five concise value angles

1. Help ready-to-book callers take a next step while your team is busy.
2. Give routine cleaning questions a consistent answer from approved business facts.
3. Let staff stay focused on jobs while routine inquiries are handled under your rules.
4. Turn a vague callback into a clear service request with useful context.
5. Keep estimates and appointment requests within boundaries you approve.

Use “designed to” or “a pilot would test” for unmeasured outcomes. Avoid “never miss a lead,” “replace your receptionist,” “24/7 guaranteed,” and “pays for itself.” Immediate response is a design aim within configured coverage, subject to routing/provider availability; it is not an SLA.

## Owner-supplied baseline

Ask for approximate counts or a short voluntary log, without collecting caller identities: eligible calls, unanswered calls, callbacks completed, typical handling minutes and appointment-request outcomes. Handling-time hypothesis = routine eligible calls × owner-estimated minutes, then compare observed pilot staff time; do not count all voice minutes as savings. If discussing economics, use owner-provided contribution per job and observed incremental completed jobs, disclose attribution uncertainty, and keep interruption-time savings separate to avoid double counting. No revenue projection is needed to ask for a demo.
